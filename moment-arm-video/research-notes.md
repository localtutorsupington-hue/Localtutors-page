# Research notes: moment arm (for the kids' video)

Technical words such as torque, perpendicular and lever are used here only. They don't appear in the script.

Method note: research used web search. Direct page fetches were blocked by this environment's network, so each fact below is backed by the search results' summaries and quotes of the listed pages. I didn't open the full pages myself.

## 1. Core physics (confirmed)

- **Moment arm**: the perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation (the joint) to the line of action of the force.
  - OpenStax University Physics Vol. 1, §10.6 Torque: "r⊥ = r sin θ is the perpendicular distance from O to the line determined by the vector F and is called the lever arm." https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/10-6-torque
  - BYU-Idaho kinesiology text, ch. 6: https://books.byui.edu/the_introduction_and_application_of_kinesiology_biomechanics_and_assessment/chapter_6
  - UCSD Muscle Physiology, "Joint Moment Arm": https://muscle.ucsd.edu/refs/musintro/ma.shtml
- **Torque = force × moment arm** (|τ| = r⊥F = rF sin θ). The torque is zero when the force line passes through the pivot (θ = 0) and largest when the force is at 90° to the segment.
  - OpenStax (above); Lumen copy: https://courses.lumenlearning.com/suny-osuniversityphysics/chapter/10-6-torque/
  - LibreTexts: https://phys.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/University_Physics/Mechanics_and_Relativity_(Idema)/05%3A_Rotational_Motion_Torque_and_Angular_Momentum/5.03%3A_Torque
- **Why it feels harder or easier**: the weight's force (gravity) is the same, but a longer moment arm means more torque. The muscle at that joint must make more torque to hold or move the weight, so the lift feels harder. A shorter moment arm means less torque, so it feels easier.
- **For a free weight, gravity pulls straight down.** The force line is vertical through the weight, so the moment arm is the **horizontal** distance from the joint to that vertical line.
  - Barbell Medicine, "Barbell forces, levers, moments and torque": https://www.barbellmedicine.com/blog/barbell-forces-levers-moments-and-torque/
- **The limb is not always the moment arm.** It equals the limb length only when the limb is at 90° to the force line. The drawing must therefore show the horizontal distance to the weight's vertical line, not just "the arm."

## 2. Kid comparison: backpack close vs far (confirmed by principle)

- Holding a load farther from the body increases the horizontal distance to the shoulder (and lower back), so the moment is larger.
  - Backpack load lifters shorten the moment arm to the lumbar spine and lower the torque: https://backpackinglight.com/dispatches/the-engineering-behind-backpack-load-lifters
  - A reach closer to the body reduced the shoulder moment (overhead drilling study, CDC/NIOSH stacks): https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/193281
- I found no single study measuring "backpack at chest vs at arm's length." The claim follows directly from the definition (force × horizontal distance) and is well supported in principle.

## 3. Exercise examples

### A. Biceps curl: elbow is the joint (confirmed)
- The dumbbell's pull is straight down. With the forearm horizontal (about 90° elbow bend), the horizontal distance from elbow to dumbbell line equals the forearm length. That's the **longest** moment arm and the hardest point.
- With the forearm close to vertical (arm hanging, or at the very top), the dumbbell line passes close to the elbow, so the moment arm is **short** and it feels easier.
  - Human Kinetics, "The mechanics of biceps exercise": https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/the-mechanics-of-biceps-exercise (UK copy: https://uk.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/the-mechanics-of-biceps-exercise)
  - NFPT: https://nfpt.com/?p=6437
  - The Movement System, CSCS levers: https://www.themovementsystem.com/blog/cscs-muscle-levers-explained
- Side note, not used in the script: a study found the *muscles'* own moment arm peaks near 75° of elbow bend, not 90°. That's about the muscle, not the weight, so it doesn't change the video's point. https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/2192/2048
- Drawing plan: "easier" uses a forearm angled about 25° from vertical, so the short line is still visible. A perfectly vertical forearm would give a zero-length line.

### B. Lateral (side) raise: shoulder is the joint (confirmed)
- Straight arm out to the side: the horizontal distance from shoulder to dumbbell line is the whole arm, so the moment arm is long and it's harder.
- Bent elbow, weight brought in: the dumbbell sits closer to the shoulder horizontally, so the moment arm is shorter and it's easier.
  - Physiotherapist quoted in Stylist: "if you bend at the elbow, it feels easier. That's because you are reducing the moment arm by bringing the weights closer to the body." https://stylist.co.uk/fitness-health/workouts/lateral-raises-hard-progress/636293
  - Livestrong, lateral raise with bent arms: https://www.livestrong.com/article/499235-lateral-raise-with-bent-arms/
  - Frontiers in Physiology 2025, dumbbell vs cable lateral raise (resistance profile): https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1611468/full
- Not used: whether bent or straight builds the shoulder better is debated. The video only says "easier" vs "harder."

### C. Squat: hip is the joint (confirmed). Deadlift is the same idea; I chose the squat.
- The bar's weight pulls straight down (over mid-foot). The hip moment arm is the horizontal distance from the hip joint to that vertical line.
- Standing tall gives a short distance. Leaning the torso far forward pushes the hips back, so the horizontal distance grows. The hip moment arm is longer, so it's harder **for the hips**.
  - Barbell Medicine (above)
  - Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 2021 (forward lean raises the hip moment arm in the sticking region): https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.691459/full
  - PMC article on squat moment arms: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8217455/
- Accuracy note: a more upright squat moves some demand to the knees, so the script says "harder for your hips," not "harder overall."

### D. Plank: knees vs toes. **[uncertain]**, left out of the script
- Fitness sources agree the knee plank is easier and say it "shortens the lever" (Hinge Health https://www.hingehealth.com/gb/en/resources/articles/plank-on-knees/ ; Fitwill https://fitwill.app/exercise/9669/plank-from-knees/).
- I found **no** measured study comparing knee and toe planks for the moment at the hip or lower back. There's also no single clean "joint plus weight line" to draw. The body is held up at both ends (like a bridge), and which joint and which force line to show depends on the model. I couldn't draw this as a simple, correct joint-to-weight-line distance, so I dropped it.

## 4. Decisions for the script
- Exercises (in research order, 3 total): **biceps curl → side raise → squat.** The plank is excluded as [uncertain].
- Every drawing: a joint dot, a dashed vertical line through the weight (its pull), and a bold horizontal line from the joint to that dashed line (the moment arm). That line is clearly longer in "Harder" than in "Easier."
- Banned on screen and in the voiceover: torque, perpendicular, lever.
